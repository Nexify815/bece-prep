# StudyBuddy — The Student's Journey

*How it feels to use StudyBuddy, from first open to exam day.*

## The story in one line

> A JHS student opens StudyBuddy, tells it their name and goal, and from then on the app quietly tells them **what to study today** — they learn the words, climb the stairs, answer questions, and watch their streak and XP grow until exam day.

---

## Chapter 1 — First visit: "What is this?"

1. Student opens the link (bece-prep.vercel.app) on a phone, possibly with patchy internet — or it's already installed to their home screen.
2. A short **splash screen** with the mascot appears for ~3 seconds (only the very first time).
3. A friendly **welcome flow** walks them through, one screen at a time:
   - *"Climb the Stairs — lessons, one unlocked step at a time"*
   - *"Learn the words — five new terms a day beats fifty you forget"*
   - *"Then test yourself"*
   - *"Fix what you missed"*
4. Then two optional questions: **"What should we call you?"** and **"Daily study goal?"** (30 / 60 / 90 / 120 minutes — the streak is measured against this).
5. A **one-time guided tour** spotlights the real home screen: today's plan card, the highlighted next step, the shortcuts, the full task list, and the streak badge. After this, the app never interrupts with tutorials again.

From here on, every visit feels like arriving at a place that knows them.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Home screen: "What should I do today?"

The home page is a warm "front door":

- **Greeting** — "Welcome back, Ama!" plus how close they are to today's goal.
- **Daily goal ring** — a circle filling toward their chosen minutes (e.g. 45 of 60 min done).
- **Pick a subject** — four colourful subject cards (Mathematics, Integrated Science, English, Social Studies) with a "42 / 120 terms learned" bar under each.
- **Today's Plan** — the heart of the app. It looks at *their own* progress and lays out today's steps: typically a lesson from their weakest subject, some terms to learn, a quiz set, and a final step (often reviewing mistakes). Only **one step is ever highlighted as "next"** — the student never has to decide, just tap the big button. Weekends are gentle "light days" with review instead of new material.
- **Question of the Day** — one quick teaser question; a small XP reward if they get it.
- The plan **rewrites itself every day** based on what they've actually done.

Above everything sits the top bar: streak flame, XP, hearts. On a phone, a bottom nav; on a laptop, a side rail.

---

## Chapter 3 — Learning the words

Tap a subject → the **subject hub** appears: progress at a glance and big doors into each mode.

**Learn mode** — like flipping through flashcards with a teacher beside you: read the term in plain language, see an example, tap "I know this" or "Still learning". Marking a term learned for the first time earns XP and fills in today's plan step. There's a read-aloud button for listening, and a mic button for speaking answers.

**Glossary** — the searchable dictionary of ~745 BECE terms. Each card shows the definition, an example sentence, and difficulty. Students can star terms as "learned", add their own custom terms, and tap to hear them read aloud. Wrong answers anywhere else resurface here for review.

**Flashcards** — quick self-testing with **spaced repetition**: the app remembers which terms they struggle with and brings those back sooner, while well-known terms fade away. Perfect for the commute or the queue.

---

## Chapter 4 — Climbing the Stairs (the signature feature)

Think of a mountain with stair steps, one per lesson of the syllabus:

1. Each step unlocks in order. A step = a short lesson teaching a handful of terms from one topic.
2. The lesson ends with a **mini-quiz**. Pass → the step is done, confetti, +50 XP.
3. Fail → the step locks until a "1-heart retry" — a nudge to actually revise, not just click through.
4. Clear all the steps and a **Summit mega-quiz** unlocks at the top — a big mixed test covering the whole subject. Pass it → +100 XP and serious bragging rights.

The stairs turn "study science" into "climb one more step" — visible, finite, and satisfying.

---

## Chapter 5 — Testing yourself

**Quiz mode** — pick Easy / Medium / Hard. The app serves short runs from questions they haven't mastered yet (a completed "set" needs every question solved). Features along the way:

- Instant feedback: green check / red cross with the explanation right there.
- **Hints** (15 XP) — removes two wrong options for multiple choice, or a hint letter for fill-in-the-blank.
- **Worked solutions** (30 XP) — a full walkthrough for questions they keep missing.
- Hearts only drop in **Challenge mode**; by default they're in **Practice mode**, where mistakes are free — the app never punishes a child for trying.

**Past Papers** — real BECE questions from 2023–2025 (80 per subject), timed, just like the real thing.

**Mock Exam** — a full simulated exam with a timer and a history of past scores, so exam day holds no surprises.

**Drill & Review Mistakes** — the "fix what you missed" step: every wrong answer lands in a mistakes bank and comes back until it's answered correctly. The plan won't let the final step complete until the bank is empty.

**Section B** — written-answer practice with self-assessment and model answers.

---

## Chapter 6 — The carrots: XP, streaks, hearts, and the store

- **XP** flows in constantly (5 per correct answer, 10 per term learned, 50 per lesson, 100 summit bonus, plus bonuses for sprints, the daily question, monthly challenges, essays). Every 100 XP = a level, celebrated with a level-up moment.
- **Streaks** — a flame in the top bar. Practice any day and it grows; miss a day and it breaks (unless a streak-freeze was bought in the store). The daily goal ring makes "today counts" visible.
- **Hearts** — five lives in Challenge mode; lose one per wrong answer, one refills every 20 minutes, or buy one back for 25 XP when broke.
- **The Store** — themes (Day/Night/Berry/Ocean), 2× XP boosts, streak freezes. (Currently all themes are free.)
- **Sprints** — "study for 10 or 15 minutes" timed sessions with their own XP payout — good for short attention windows.
- **Progress Report** — a printable, saveable report card of everything: terms learned, quiz bests, streak, mock history, time studied. Something to show parents, a teacher, or just yourself.

And it all happens **offline** — on the bus, at home with no Wi-Fi, deep in the syllabus. Progress saves on the device automatically.

---

## Chapter 7 — Keeping it safe (and optionally, everywhere)

- Every change is saved on the device instantly — no login needed, ever.
- A **backup** can be exported as a text code or a JSON file and restored on another phone/laptop — a safety net for device loss.
- Optionally, the student creates a **username + 6-digit PIN account** (no email needed). Then progress syncs in real time between devices: start a quiz on a school computer, finish it on the phone at home — it just continues.
- A friendly nudge reminds them to back up every so often (dismissible, and never shown to signed-in users).

---

## Chapter 8 — Exam mode: the final stretch

When exams loom, **Exam Mode** switches the app into cram gear — entirely separate from XP/hearts:

1. Enter the **exam date** and pick the subjects.
2. Rate confidence per subject (1–5) and mark each syllabus topic **"know it / unsure / blank"** — the app builds a checklist.
3. Choose a mode: **Paper** (full timed past paper), **Blitz** (rapid-fire weak spots), **Weak** (only the "unsure/blank" topics), **Checklist** (tick off the syllabus), or **Cram** (focused final-week plan).
4. Every session is logged, so they can watch readiness actually climb as the exam approaches.

## The emotional arc, summarized

| Stage | What the student feels |
|---|---|
| First open | "Oh, it just tells me what to do." |
| First session | "I learned 5 words and the step unlocked — nice." |
| One week in | "I can't break my 9-day streak now." |
| Quiz time | "I know which questions I always miss — and my XP bought a night theme." |
| Mistakes | "It only asks me what I got wrong. Annoying but it works." |
| Exam week | "Everything's marked know-it / unsure — I can see exactly where I stand." |
| Exam day | Calm. They've rehearsed this exam dozens of times. |
